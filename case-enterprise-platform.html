K–12 High-Stakes Assessment Cloud & AI Platform

From complex enterprise and RFP requirements to a scalable cloud platform, phased global execution, and a multi-year business outcome.

The challenge

Enterprise K–12 assessment programs bring together complex workflows, contractual requirements, high-stakes delivery dates, compliance constraints, and extreme reliability expectations. The product challenge was to turn that complexity into a roadmap the engineering team could actually execute—without solving every customer request as a one-off customization.

What I owned

  • Led product strategy and roadmap for a mission-critical K–12 assessment cloud platform.
  • Led a ~20-person distributed/global engineering team across product priorities, Agile delivery, scalability, and production risk.
  • Worked directly with enterprise customers through discovery, onboarding, requirements workshops, demos, and issue resolution.
  • Participated in the RFP process and helped translate business and contractual commitments into feasible product plans.
  • Partnered with engineering and executive stakeholders on APIs/integrations, scalability, authentication/RBAC, least-privilege access, student PII protection, and platform tradeoffs.

From complex requirements to execution

Customer / RFP→Clarify need→Product capability→Prioritize→Epics / stories→Sprint & release→Production + feedback

My role was to make large requirements actionable: separate the customer outcome from the requested implementation, translate capabilities into Jira-managed epics and user stories, define acceptance criteria and dependencies, and align phased releases with engineering capacity and business commitments.

The key product tradeoff

Enterprise deals can create pressure to promise exactly what one customer requests. But excessive customization can fragment the platform and increase long-term delivery cost.

ApproachBenefitRisk
Customer-specific customizationDirectly addresses the immediate requestFragmentation and maintenance burden
Wait for a fully generic platform solutionCleaner long-term architectureCan miss customer timing and business commitments
Reusable capability + phased delivery ✓Meets the underlying need while strengthening the platformRequires disciplined scope and stakeholder alignment
Decision principleUse customer and RFP requirements as inputs—not the architecture. Clarify the underlying workflow, identify reusable patterns, then prioritize a phased solution that balances customer value, feasibility, delivery risk, and long-term platform strategy.

What I learned

Simplifying complexity is a product skill.Large enterprise requirements become manageable when the PM can identify the underlying user and business outcome, separate must-haves from implementation preferences, expose dependencies and tradeoffs, and give engineering a sequence of clear decisions rather than a large document to “build.”
