The product problem

AI teams can build convincing demos long before they can answer the question that matters in production: what evidence is sufficient to release this system? Aggregate accuracy alone can hide high-severity failures such as hallucinated answers, unsafe tool use, broken fallback behavior, excessive latency, or unreliable escalation.

Product thesisEvaluation should not end in a dashboard. It should produce an actionable, risk-based deployment decision that product and engineering teams can defend.
Why teams struggleRelease evidence is fragmented across manual tests, dashboards, engineering judgment, and one-off evaluations.
What the product changesOne repeatable workflow connects failures to severity, remediation priorities, readiness criteria, and a go/no-go decision.
Intended valueSurface high-risk failures earlier, focus engineering effort, strengthen regression decisions, and make release risk easier to explain.

What I would measure

DimensionExample signalsWhy it matters
QualityTask success, groundedness, factuality, retrieval qualityA response can be fluent while still being wrong or unsupported.
SafetyPolicy violations, unsafe tool actions, sensitive-data handlingSome failures should block release regardless of average quality.
ReliabilityTool/API failures, fallback success, state consistency, escalationProduction systems must recover when dependencies fail.
ExperienceLatency, escalation rate, user completionTechnically correct behavior can still create a poor product experience.
EconomicsCost per successful task, token/tool usageQuality improvements must remain economically sustainable at scale.

The PM judgment: not every failure is equal

Example:A 95% overall success rate may look launch-ready. But if the remaining 5% contains unauthorized tool actions or high-impact hallucinations, the correct product decision can still be NO-GO.

I would define release gates by severity and use case rather than relying on one universal score. A low-risk internal assistant can tolerate a different failure profile than an agent taking actions on behalf of a customer.

Customer engineering → core product loop

For a Forward Deployed PM, deployment is not a handoff. The highest-value work happens at the boundary between the customer's engineering environment and the core product team.

1. Observe in the fieldIntegration path, data constraints, failure modes, latency and operational friction
2. Debug with customer engineersSeparate configuration issues, product gaps, model behavior, and dependency failures
3. Translate into product decisionsReproducible scenarios, requirements, severity, tradeoffs, and acceptance criteria
4. Feed the core platformTurn recurring deployment needs into reusable capabilities—not one-off customizations
My role at the boundaryKeep the customer outcome, technical reality, and long-term product strategy aligned: understand enough of the integration to unblock the field, then convert what we learn into a scalable product decision for engineering.

What I would build next

The next step is to make readiness profiles use-case-specific and regression-aware. Teams should be able to compare a new model, prompt, retrieval configuration, or tool version against an approved baseline and immediately understand what changed, which failures matter, and whether the system remains safe to release.

Approved baseline→Model / prompt / tool change→Regression eval→Risk delta→Release decision
